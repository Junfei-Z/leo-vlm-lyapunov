% ============ MAIN CLAIM (mainline subsection, replaces min-fill-optimality) ====
Across the studied battery range the proposed scheduler is the only policy
that simultaneously (i) meets the availability SLA at every operating point
where any policy meets it, (ii) sustains the split-only 7B tier (the only
deployable splitter: every other splitting policy violates the SLA wherever
it splits at scale), and (iii) carries the ISS and $O(1/V)$ total-quality
guarantees of Section~V. Each component of this claim is robust to the
quality-mapping upgrade (the B1 anchor verified all three on measured
per-class values) and none depends on the specific Q-values. Min-fill, by
contrast, belongs to no single policy: the deployable min-fill leader
migrates with the energy regime -- at [15]\,kJ the proposed scheduler is the
only deployable policy at all; at the rule-sized point the non-adaptive 3B
policy leads (0.194 versus 0.165, with double the downtime); from
[19]\,kJ Random leads (0.336) as energy abundance removes the constraint
that scheduling exists to manage. The proposed scheduler stays within
[15]\% of the deployable leader through the tight and middle band and
delivers [59--70]\% of Random's min-fill in abundance, at the lowest
downtime of any policy at every point. We report this migration as a
finding, and the evidence for it is cumulative: the leader moved when the
battery size moved (the deployability band), moved again when the stylized
quality mapping was replaced by measured per-class values, and differs in
all three energy regimes -- min-fill under energy scarcity is a property of
the regime, not of any scheduler.

% ============ STATIC FINDING (own paragraph, mainline; regime-dependence) ====
The strong showing of the non-adaptive 3B policy at the rule-sized point is
itself informative, and it is trace-structural: under the simultaneous
eclipse of the mainline the reserve guard idles the proposed scheduler on
[2304] of [7963] decision opportunities, and a policy that simply ignores
the eclipse forecast -- serving until the battery protection trips and
paying [3.2]\% downtime -- converts the difference into service volume. On
the phase-staggered contrast schedule the ordering reverts: the proposed
scheduler's fairness end (min-fill [0.244]) dominates Static ([0.198])
outright, because phase diversity restores the service windows the guard
withholds under simultaneous eclipse -- with its schedule no longer idled,
the proposed scheduler serves enough volume that Static's
ignore-the-forecast dividend vanishes. The guard's cost is
regime-dependent, which the limitations discussion takes up.

Three independent measurements now triangulate the same conservatism. First,
the rate-based energy queue is blind to the state of charge (idle
decomposition: [2649] score idles at the mainline point). Second, the
eclipse-reserve guard dominates the idle budget under simultaneous eclipse
([2304] of [7963] opportunities blocked) and compresses the navigable $V$
range. Third, two opponents expose what the conservatism leaves on the
table, from opposite directions: the non-adaptive 3B policy spends the
safety margin outright -- no lookahead, downtime [3.2]\% -- and converts it
into service volume ([0.194] min-fill, [5576] total quality); orbit-window
MPC, by contrast, needs no extra downtime at all ([1.59]\% on both) and
still lifts min-fill to [0.269] purely by planning across the full
sunlit--eclipse cycle -- fairness that was available inside the same safety
envelope, which the per-slot rule with a fixed-fraction reserve cannot
reach. A guard that grades its reserve by the state of charge is the direct
design implication for the margin side; the lookahead side is measurably
out of reach of any $O(1)$ per-slot rule (Section~[EV_MPC]).

% ============ FUTURE WORK SENTENCE (conclusion; two-line structure with
% measured boundaries -- the SoC-aware expected contribution is COMPRESSED
% by the ablation: it can fix over-withholding, not the lookahead premium) ====
The two evidence lines bound two distinct improvement directions. An
SoC-aware guard addresses the over-withholding that the Static comparison
and the contrast-schedule reversal expose -- its expected gain is bounded by
what margin-spending recovers. It does not reach the orbit-lookahead
premium: the window ablation of Section~[EV_MPC] shows that premium requires
planning across the full sunlit--eclipse cycle even with full SoC access,
guard exemption, and equal downtime ($W{=}$half MPC does not outperform the
proposed scheduler) -- closing that part of the gap within $O(1)$ per-slot
cost is an open question.
