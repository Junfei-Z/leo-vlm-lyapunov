\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}\label{EV_SENS}
We sweep the scenario parameters one at a time around the mainline point, each
value over 10 seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:sens}).

The platform-baseline sweep exposes the 7B feasibility boundary: the split is
feasible only while $P_{\text{base}} + 6.37 \le 15$\,W, below about 8.6\,W. At
6\,W every policy is comfortable and Greedy-Q dominates min-fill while
deployable; at the nominal 7\,W the regime is tight; at 8\,W all policies
degrade and beyond 9\,W the platform load alone is unsustainable (the
${\approx}7.8$\,W orbit-average harvest is exceeded) and every policy
collapses. The battery sweep bounds the scope of the safety separation on the
real trace: Greedy-Q is undeployable across the entire studied range
($\ge 6.9\%$ for $B^{\max}\!\in\![14,22]$\,kJ), the strict three-in/two-out
partition holds for $B^{\max}\!\in\![16.6, 17.5]$\,kJ, and Random becomes
deployable and overtakes on min-fill from ${\sim}19$\,kJ (0.313 versus 0.214),
the energy-abundant regime of the limitations discussion. The panel sweep
shows the same story from the supply side (partition on $[12.2, 13]$\,W;
sustainability floor near 11\,W). Under overload the proposed scheduler's
downtime stays flat at 1.6\% up to a 0.7 arrival probability while every
policy's min-fill falls. At the mainline point the idle decomposition
attributes the scheduler's conservatism as 2306 reserve-guard blocks and 2661
score idles of 7961 decision slots: under simultaneous eclipse the guard of
Remark~\ref{rem_reserve} is the dominant term, the mechanism behind the
compressed $V$ range of Table~\ref{tab_split}.

\subsection{Dynamic ISL Topology}\label{EV_ISL}
To stress the handoff constraint~\eqref{C_pipe_isl} beyond the always-connected
chain, we degrade link availability with a per-window duty cycle
$p_{\text{ISL}}$ on top of the real geometry (Fig.~\ref{fig:isl}). As
availability falls from 1.0 to 0.3 the split count falls from 655 to 333 and
min-fill from 0.176 to 0.113. The coupling has a verified linear structure:
the loss concentrates entirely on the 7B-dependent source in every setting
(all other sources rise slightly as standalone capacity flows back), and the
fill loss per lost split is the same constant (0.020 per 100 splits) on the
mainline and on the cross-plane contrast schedule; the larger mainline drop is
therefore not a different mechanism but four times more lost splits, because
the source is served almost entirely through splits concentrated in the
guard-allowed windows, which leave no rerouting slack. A superlinear
window-intersection mechanism we had hypothesized is rejected by this
constant-slope structure.

\subsection{Stability Stress Tests}\label{EV_STAB}
A twenty-orbit run probes the guarantees on the real trace
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stab20}). The battery settles into a periodic cycle and the
virtual queues remain mean-rate stable ($Q^E(T)/T \to 1.6\times10^{-3}$,
$Q^U(T)/T \to 7.9\times10^{-3}$; blackout 0.24\% of satellite-slots). The
backlogs are larger than on the staggered contrast schedule (peak mean
backlogs 239 and 950 versus 86 and 192): this is the ISS bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm_robust} responding to the longer uninterrupted
positive-drift interval of simultaneous eclipse, not a departure from it --
the disturbance is larger, so the stability bound is larger, and the queues
remain sublinear throughout.
