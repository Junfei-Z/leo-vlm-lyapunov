\subsection{Comparison with Model-Predictive Control}\label{EV_MPC}
We benchmark against a model-predictive controller built to be the strongest
fair opponent: a windowed MILP over $\Delta{=}30$\,s macro-slots that receives
the same deterministic schedules the proposed scheduler can compute offline
(sunlit, ISL, eclipse forecast) and the same causal state, with future
arrivals entering only through the rate $\lambda$; it carries the same
cross-window fairness memory (window objective $\max U$ s.t.\ $U \le D_i +
\sum_{\text{window}} q_i$ with $D_i$ the negative of the $Q^U_i$ content); it
is exempt from the eclipse-reserve guard, with battery safety left to its own
optimization through a terminal condition (end-of-window charge must cover the
platform to the next sunrise) and a planning-layer relaxation of the battery
floor whose penalty (10 quality units per Joule) exceeds any action's
quality-per-Joule by $43\times$. The relaxation exists only in the planning
layer: at execution every MPC action passes the same shared feasibility
oracle and the same safe-mode blackout accounting as every baseline. Plans
re-solve every $R{=}60$\,s (CBC, 0.5\% MIP gap) under two declared regimes:
\emph{real-time}, where the solve is capped at $R$ and the new plan takes
effect only after the measured solve wall-clock has elapsed (the previous
plan keeps executing meanwhile), and \emph{oracle}, where solve time is not
charged to the simulation clock, an upper bound. Windows $W \in
\{\text{half-eclipse } 1035\,\mathrm{s}, \text{eclipse } 2070\,\mathrm{s},
\text{orbit } 5736\,\mathrm{s}\}$; 10 seeds throughout.

% ---- flip narrative: the fairness premium belongs to orbit-scale lookahead ----
The window sweep turns the comparison from a verdict into a mechanism
(Table~\ref{tab_mpc}). With a half-eclipse window the MILP, holding full SoC
visibility and the full per-window optimization machinery, does \emph{not}
outperform the per-slot scheduler (min-fill 0.166 versus 0.174); with an
eclipse-length window it ties (0.176); only when the window covers the full
sunlit--eclipse cycle does a fairness premium open (0.265, with total quality
3521 versus 2141). The premium is therefore attached to orbit-scale
lookahead, not to SoC visibility or optimization power at matched horizons.
Its price is measured by the same experiment: per-decision compute
$4.5\times10^{3}$--$7.6\times10^{4}\times$ the proposed scheduler's
$27\,\mu$s per-slot rule, a heavy-tailed solve-time distribution whose
$\tau{=}1$\,s exceedance grows from 0\% (half) through 5.3\% (eclipse) to
36\% (orbit, p95 $8.7$--$9.7$\,s, max 300\,s), and a reliance on a MILP
solver on-board. The real-time and oracle regimes coincide throughout (the
60\,s incumbent is already near-optimal, 0 incumbent failures, no solve with
MIP gap above 50\%), so the orbit-tier premium is real rather than an
artifact of uncharged solve time; by the pre-registered criterion every tier
remains usable at the real-time budget on the workstation.

% ---- amortization caliber sentence ----
Two compute columns answer two different questions: amortized per executed
slot the ratio shrinks mechanically with $W$ (one solve per 60 slots against
a rule run every slot, $74\times$ at $W{=}$half), which measures the average
compute bill; the per-decision figure and the $\tau$-exceedance fraction
measure whether a decision is available \emph{on time}, and on-board
scheduling is the latter problem -- a missed replanning cycle is a real
failure mode, an average bill is not.

% ---- phase mechanism (orbit only; eclipse reported as heavy tail, no mechanism) ----
For the orbit tier the hard instances cluster at eclipse boundaries (median
distance 163 slots versus 355 for all solves): the solves are slowest
precisely when timely replanning matters most. The eclipse tier's tail shows
no such clustering (638 versus 356) and is reported as heavy-tailed without a
mechanism claim.

% ---- Jetson paragraph (paired re-solve, pre-registered sampling) ----
On the target device (Jetson Orin NX) we re-solve, per tier, ten windows
drawn by a pre-registered rule (eight octile-median instances plus the two
hardest) and pair them with unloaded workstation re-solves of the identical
instances, both at the real-time budget (60\,s cap, 0.5\% gap). The verdict
is carried by the body of the distribution, not the tail: on the device the
orbit tier exceeds $\tau$ on \emph{all ten} instances (fastest 1.75\,s,
mean 7.5\,s) -- the only window that wins fairness is never per-slot
real-time on-board -- while half crosses on 1/10 and eclipse on 3/10. The
distribution body transfers at ${\sim}3\times$ (device/workstation);
per-instance hardness in the tail does not transfer at all (the
workstation's hardest eclipse instance, 300\,s in the batch, solves in
1.3\,s on the device, while a mid-stratum orbit instance grows from 3.3\,s
to 24.3\,s): branch-and-bound trees are architecture-fragile, which is
itself a deployment liability for on-board MILP and the reason the
$\tau$-exceedance fraction, not any per-instance figure, is the declared
real-time criterion.

% ---- limitation corroboration (hook into the limitations discussion) ----
% To be appended to the reserve-guard limitation pillar:
The orbit-tier MPC also corroborates the guard's conservatism from the
opponent's side: an optimizer permitted to spend the eclipse reserve chose a
more aggressive energy policy (downtime 2.39\% versus 1.6\%, still inside the
SLA) and converted it into min-fill 0.26 on all ten seeds.

% ---- precise future-work sentence (conclusion / limitations) ----
An SoC-aware guard may recover part of the fairness gap; however, the window
sweep shows the premium is attached to orbit-scale lookahead rather than SoC
visibility alone ($W{=}$half MPC, with full SoC access, does not outperform
the proposed scheduler) -- closing the full gap within $O(1)$ per-slot cost
is an open question.
