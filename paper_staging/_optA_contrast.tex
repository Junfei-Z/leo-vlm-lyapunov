\subsection{Cross-Plane Contrast: the Conflict Under Phase Diversity}\label{EV_VCONF}
This subsection is a controlled counterpart approximating a cross-plane
deployment, where eclipse-phase diversity exists: the schedule staggers the
four eclipse phases synthetically (0.60 sunlit fraction; sizing by the same
rules, 13\,W and 18\,kJ; the handoff band ends are absorbed regardless).
Phase diversity restores the room that simultaneous eclipse removes, and two
things become visible that the mainline compresses. First, the
fairness-throughput conflict itself: sweeping the Lyapunov weight traces a
genuine frontier (Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier}), from a fairness end (min-fill
0.175 at $V{=}1$, exceeding Greedy-E's 0.156 by $5\sigma$ at comparable total
quality) to a throughput end (3850 at $V{=}300$); the policies that reach both
high fairness and high throughput (Greedy-Q 0.398, Random 0.354) do so only by
blacking out (7.0\% and 5.3\%), and the partition is invariant for any SLA in
$(0.8\%, 5.3\%)$. Second, the $V$-space signature of the conflict
(Fig.~\ref{fig:vconf}): total quality rises with $V$, so the $O(1/V)$
improvement of Theorem~\ref{thm_gap} holds, while min-fill falls, reversing
the conflict-free single-satellite behaviour; the reversal does not violate
Theorem~\ref{thm_gap}, which bounds total quality rather than min-fill.
Comparing the two settings: the graded picture survives contact with the real
trace unchanged (reckless splitters undeployable, the proposed scheduler the
only deployable splitter and best min-fill among deployables), while two
things tighten -- the strict deployability partition narrows from a wide band
to essentially the rule-derived sizes, and the $V$-navigable range compresses
because the reserve guard dominates under simultaneous eclipse. On this
schedule the constellation-size axis is also visible: at 16 satellites the
same guard blocks about 2.7 times more decision slots than at 8, producing a
non-monotone min-fill (the serial one-decision-per-slot implementation is the
corresponding limitation).
