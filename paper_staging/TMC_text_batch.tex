% ---------- (3) Methodology paragraph (lead of the trace-driven subsection):
\subsection{Trace-Driven Verification on Real Ephemeris}\label{EV_TLE}
The synthetic schedule above fixes the sunlit fraction and staggers the eclipse
phases; both are approximations. To test what survives contact with a real
constellation, we replace the schedule with traces propagated from a public
Starlink TLE snapshot (SGP4, 550\,km shell, 1\,s steps): a physically gated
pipeline selects four truly co-orbiting satellites of one plane (neighbor
ranges 1800\,km, stable over the window; ISL availability 1.0) from the
deepest-eclipse plane that admits a connected chain. The sizing rules of
Section~\ref{EV_SETUP} are re-applied unchanged to the trace (panel =
sustainability floor $\times1.114$, battery = eclipse need $\times1.128$), and
the full battery and panel sweeps are re-run. We report what survived, what
tightened, and why. Two structural facts emerge before any scheduling runs.
First, intra-plane phase staggering and ISL connectivity are mutually
exclusive: satellites a quarter-orbit apart are ${\sim}12{,}000$\,km apart with
Earth in between, so a physically consistent single-plane chain consists of
adjacent satellites, and eclipse then hits the whole chain nearly
simultaneously. Any scheduling design that assumes a sunlit neighbor is always
available inherits an assumption that does not hold within a plane. Second, the
synthetic staggered schedule is therefore best read as an approximation of a
cross-plane deployment, where phase diversity exists but the links crossing
planes are exactly the intermittent ones (Section~\ref{EV_ISL}).

\subsection{Measured Split Handoff}\label{EV_HANDOFF}
We execute the 7B split on the Jetson with the rear stage behind a real RPC
boundary and shape the link in userspace to an $(R, D)$ ISL model (rates
$\{0.1, 1, 10\}$\,Gbps, one-way delays $\{2, 5, 15\}$\,ms; the shaper affects
only the RPC connection, and the model loads unshaped). The split output
matches the monolithic model exactly (20/20). Two measured facts structure the
cost. First, traffic: the prefill handoff is a constant $4.085$\,MB per
inference and each decoded token adds $0.449$\,MB, of which the activation
itself is only $1.23$\,MB and $7$\,KB respectively; the remainder is the RPC
implementation (vocabulary logits and protocol), so an activation-only
protocol would compress the traffic about $3\times$. Second, latency: fitting
the nine shaped tiers yields ${\sim}56$ protocol round trips per inference, so
the delay term ($\mathrm{RTT}\times$round-trips) dominates the transfer term
at laser-class rates. At the intra-plane delays of our measured chain
(2--6\,ms one-way) the net handoff is $0.25$--$0.69$\,s: it exceeds the 5\%
threshold pre-registered for parameter recalibration, so we account it in the
split timing (band ends reported), while the one-slot structural threshold is
crossed only at cross-plane delays (15\,ms: $1.7$\,s), where the handoff
consumes an additional slot; both effects are consistent with the intra- versus
cross-plane structure of Section~\ref{EV_TLE}.
